# EP fulfilment follow-up: observed source and UAT gap

The approved public/demo handoff (`uat/workflows.json`, `uat/plugin-semantics.json` and `uat/README.md`, in the SHA-256-pinned archive referenced in `QUALIFICATION.md`) contains 15 published workflow records. Each describes an `order_completed` trigger, an immediately configured `send_email` action, and a transactional message. The Core Module workflow for source offer 171 includes a download link in its email body. Other workflows include material and booking links. The UAT handoff replaces actual paid links with the supplied dummy Dropbox transfer and disables sending.

These records establish **configured source behavior**, not proof that every email was sent or delivered, that its destination currently works, or how long an actual legacy link remains usable. The dummy Transfer has its own expiry and must not be used to infer the lifetime of production links. No customer emails, private fulfilment workbook, live WordPress database, or real paid content were inspected.

The source snapshot also marks both tested products, 131 and 171, as virtual and downloadable. Their WooCommerce download-limit and expiry fields are `-1` (unlimited in the source representation); the captured store options permit downloads without login, grant access after payment, and use the force-download method. WooCommerce's documented native behavior, when a downloadable file and permission are present, is to expose links on the order-received page and in the order email, with account access where applicable ([WooCommerce downloadable-product guide](https://woocommerce.com/document/digital-downloadable-product-handling/)). That documentation and the captured configuration describe the expected behavior; custom templates, effective email settings and actual delivery were not verified against a live order. WooCommerce access limits are separate from the expiry or revocation of a downstream Dropbox link.

The current CartRay UAT demonstrated a confirmed test order and shows the dummy link on a browser confirmation page. Its local purchaser context expires after 24 hours; a different browser was denied. It does not send a durable transactional delivery email, provide cross-device recovery, or provide an operator-supported resend. A browser crash alone does not prove loss because browsers may restore state, but lost browser storage, device replacement or context expiry can make the local link inaccessible. Treating the confirmation page as a replacement for an email would therefore leave a customer-recovery gap.

## Required before any real EP fulfilment cutover

Preserve a customer-accessible way to recover purchased material independently of the original browser. The eventual authorised EP fulfilment integration must at minimum:

1. Bind each source-approved fulfilment workflow and intended recipient to the authoritative confirmed purchase. Preserve distinct workflows, including the source order-level `(purchaseKey, workflowKey)` overlap rule; do not merge workflows merely because dummy links happen to match.
2. Deliver the appropriate transactional link or equivalent recoverable entitlement through an approved channel, and keep a way for the customer or authorised support to retrieve or resend it after browser/device loss. Use only approved test recipients and dummy payloads during UAT.
3. Exercise duplicate settlement, overlapping bundles and retries without duplicate fulfilment; handle provider failure and uncertain acceptance without blind repeated email. Verify the experience from a second browser/device after the original context is lost.
4. Qualify actual link lifetime, expiry/replacement and any source-specific delivery or booking details against approved production mappings. Show a clear recovery path if a link is no longer usable.

The first test can use a local mailbox sink and dummy content. A real provider, recipient authority, operational recovery and live content require separate approval and qualification. This is a concrete customer-experience gate for EP fulfilment, not a request to reproduce AutomateWoo's general workflow engine. The existing WordPress process remains authoritative until a separately approved cutover.
