# CartRay — General-purpose fulfilment and channel continuity

**Status:** Planning and independent review only. No implementation or activation is authorised by this file.  
**Planning baseline:** `mitchins/cart-ray` main `1da72263f9842ca6d6be3749908d8bf2e3b400bc` (19 November 2026). PR #85 is separate and remains under review.  
**Scope:** Two independently reviewable development PRs; Effective Policy is a configuration and later qualification target, not a core dependency. Production rollout and real paid-file delivery require separate authorisation.

## Existing boundaries to reuse

- `src/cartray/post_purchase.py` consumes confirmed-order outbox records, binds merchant/environment and identifies executions by `(merchant, environment, order_id, workflow_key)`. Its configuration and hosted email handler are currently test-only; this plan does not claim production readiness. Existing configuration digests prevent reinterpretation of executions, and claimed/uncertain outcomes require deliberate recovery.
- `migrations/0006_post_purchase_actions.sql` through `0008_post_purchase_recovery.sql` provide execution, checkpoint and test-only operator reconciliation. Keep the checkout transaction independent of consumers and do not repurpose another subscriber's acknowledgement.
- `src/cartray/catalogue.py` currently represents checkout-authorised products and includes private fulfilment-resource references in the immutable order snapshot. Its Stripe price requirements make it unsuitable as the entire public channel catalogue. `src/cartray/merchant_contract.py` validates presentation routes; it is not an authority for price, delivery, or external channel IDs.
- `modules/post-purchase/transactional-email/worker.mjs` is a private, test-scoped delivery adapter, not a reusable provider-neutral entitlement or production mail service. Reuse the trusted service and execution boundary; do not elevate the example configuration into merchant authority.

## A. Fulfilment: proposed contract

**Separate the records.** A merchant-approved resource definition has a stable merchant-scoped `resource_id`, provider key, private provider-specific configuration reference, display filename/description, MIME type when known, access policy and version. A separately stored binding maps an offer and/or workflow to an ordered list of these definitions. Define whether a resource shared by two purchased offers appears once per workflow or as separate grants; preserve independent workflow identities even where targets match. The existing immutable order snapshot stores `fulfilment_resources_json`, but the dispatcher currently matches order items against current workflow configuration; the example email handler can send every configured link after any offer match. Extend the existing checkout-time snapshot narrowly to pin the workflow revision and ordered offer-to-resource associations, retain the referenced revisions, and grant only resources bound to purchased offers. Do not change payment settlement semantics. A later merchant edit must not silently change an existing execution or entitlement. Do not put private locators, provider credentials or raw capabilities into public catalogue output, browser-controlled fields, public logs, or channel feeds.

An entitlement is a durable record scoped by merchant, environment, confirmed purchase, workflow and resource identity, with its pinned resource version, policy, status and revocation/expiry timestamps if configured. Commit idempotent entitlement grants and their notification intent atomically before claiming the irreversible email send; claiming a workflow alone is not proof grants exist. The existing workflow claim can precede these grants, so explicitly add a safely resumable local-preparation stage or create the grant and intent in the same D1 transaction as its claim. Recovery must distinguish an interrupted local preparation from a possibly accepted external send; the latter remains held rather than retried. Its creation is idempotent and atomic at the appropriate D1 transaction boundary; a unique resource binding must not collapse different workflows or different purchased resources just because their underlying URL is equal. A quantity affects entitlement only where an explicit merchant policy says so. A customer capability is an opaque, unguessable bearer credential bound to the entitlement or approved purchase resource set; store a digest, not the credential. A digest-only token cannot be reconstructed after a crash: mint at the notification boundary, retain the pending intent and on a proven-unsent recovery issue a replacement generation that invalidates the lost token. If provider acceptance cannot be determined, hold for operator reconciliation; do not guess or resend. A link may be forwarded: it proves possession, not personal identity. Implement the currently missing customer access endpoint as part of this slice, using existing service composition rather than a new general platform. A GET/HEAD to an access page must not consume the entitlement. Per-request access checks enforce current revocation and policy before serving content.

**Provider adapter interface.** Define a small trusted server-side registry: an adapter receives an already-authorised entitlement and private configuration, and returns a typed access result or a scoped failure. It never accepts a browser locator or decides whether an order was paid. Adapters do not send mail. Implement:

1. `external_link`: server-configured HTTPS links, validated against a merchant-approved policy; preserve exact approved targets where required. It can redirect or present the link; the third-party provider controls its own availability and whether its link can actually be revoked. Do not claim CartRay can revoke a copied external link.
2. `private_object`: private object-store binding, with R2 as one adapter. The persistent emailed CartRay capability authorises a request each time; a private Worker streams the object after validation. Streaming is the v1 path: an already-issued provider URL can outlive a revocation and expose object details, so an optional later presign path requires an explicitly tested revocation-delay policy. A single expiring R2 presigned URL is not a durable customer link (R2 permits at most seven days). Preserve approved filename and MIME safely; guard against path/header injection, and do not make buckets public.
3. A synthetic adapter, in tests, registered through the same interface without editing the dispatcher. It returns a distinct provider result, not an R2/Dropbox alias.

**Notification remains separate.** Once entitlement creation is durable, a merchant-configured notification handler can render the resolved access information. Provider lookup failure is recorded per resource; it must not revoke or suppress independent entitlements. The default message contains stable CartRay access links for each independently granted resource; it does not probe object storage as a prerequisite for sending. An unavailable provider therefore affects only access to that resource and does not prevent unrelated links working. Do not silently report an incomplete entitlement set as success; explicitly hold only the affected notification if the grants themselves cannot be created. The ordinary handler must not auto-resend an ambiguously accepted message. Record provider reference and operator evidence separately from entitlement state. Existing `(merchant, environment, purchase, workflow)` claims and activation/no-backfill rules remain in force.

**Access lifetime and recovery.** Import the source-approved access policy as configuration; no implicit short expiry. A stable capability or equivalent recovery mechanism must continue to work from another browser after checkout/session closure, within that policy. The new access endpoint on the existing service will handle token revocation and private-object access; provider-side changes may make an external link inaccessible and must be surfaced as such. Receipt replay and repeated access do not create another entitlement or email. A revoked or expired entitlement denies private-object access even if an old capability is presented. Define the transfer and revocation limits in merchant-facing documentation.

### PR A: execution and qualification

1. Write the smallest resource/entitlement and provider-result contracts, migration and static trusted provider registry around the existing dispatcher. Register adapters by key at the access-service composition root; use common stream, redirect and unavailable outcomes, without provider branches in the dispatcher, access route or mail renderer. Pin resource definitions and match them to confirmed immutable order items and workflows. Do not create another event system.
2. Implement external-link, private-object/R2 and synthetic adapters, plus generic notification integration and operator inspection/reconciliation using existing delivery claims. Keep checkout, production sending and collection off; start with dummy content.
3. Test two merchants, multiple files, multiple links, mixed adapters and overlapping workflows including equal URLs. Check exact filenames/MIME, new-browser repeated access, unpurchased-resource denial, cross-merchant isolation, revoked/expired access, source-approved non-expiring access, capability leakage, replay and one provider unavailable while unrelated resources remain available. Test a v1 purchase drained after a v2 configuration change, A alone, A+B with shared resources, and a crash both before sending and after ambiguous provider acceptance. The synthetic third provider must work through the ordinary access endpoint without dispatcher or mail-renderer changes. Rehearse provider timeout after acceptance and operator recovery without automatic resend.
4. Exercise real Workerd/D1, private test object storage and approved sandbox email where authorised. Verify full ordinary purchase → confirmed event → entitlements → one delivery and cross-browser access; simulate denial and outage. Test refunds/cancellations only to the extent the merchant's explicit access policy and authoritative signals support them; do not silently invent policy.
5. Run existing CartRay/Reviews regressions and normal CI/security gates. Obtain exact-head independent review. Report the test topology, provider evidence, operational commands and any unqualified provider-specific revocation semantics.

## B. Channel continuity: proposed contract

A versioned, merchant-scoped **listing projection** is separate from checkout and fulfilment. Each entry has an explicit stable merchant product/variation identity, an independently assigned stable channel-specific item ID and its account/catalogue scope, a separately recorded approved SEO canonical URL, the exact submitted item or variant landing URL, title, description, primary public image URL, explicit price/currency and availability when required, variant-group identity/attributes, publication and channel eligibility, and a bounded set of adapter-owned fields. An explicit mapping relates an authorised checkout offer only when one exists; it cannot be inferred from the existence of a listing. No channel ID is generated from a Woo ID, migration artifact or CartRay key. If an already-published external ID happens to contain a Woo ID, preserve that exact approved external ID verbatim; do not generate a new one. A single SEO canonical may cover several distinct variant landing URLs, including query parameters. Preserve exact source-approved submitted URL and image strings, including slash/case/query where the verified channel contract requires them; the existing route validator applies to internally owned routes, while channel URL validation must not silently normalize an external identity or landing URL.

A channel adapter takes an immutable approved projection and returns a deterministic versioned feed artifact plus a validation report. An API synchronisation adapter is deferred until a specific merchant requirement justifies it. Google Merchant Center and Meta are adapters outside the commerce kernel. Start with a static feed and platform-scheduled retrieval where sufficient; do not add a feed Worker, polling loop or scheduler by default. Track destination-specific eligibility rather than assuming that a digital product or service is allowed for every channel and ad destination. In particular, Google distinguishes Shopping ads and free listings for digital books/PDFs; source-approved catalog inclusion is not proof of destination eligibility. Meta rules and account-specific capabilities need authoritative account evidence before activation.

**Continuity comparison.** Import an authorised current channel snapshot and record its provenance, account/catalogue/source ID, extraction time and coverage. Compare it with the proposed projection for item ID, exact landing/image URL, price/currency, availability, grouping and eligibility/publication. Report added, removed, changed, missing and unverified values, including source conflicts; do not equate missing evidence with approval or silently drop items. Bind any exception to a reviewer, reason and specific snapshot/version. Never store credentials or customer information in the public feed or report.

**Transition and rollback.** Stage an immutable feed and produce a diff before any source change. Google product identity is scoped by `(account, contentLanguage, feedLabel, offerId)`; data source, market and destination are provenance or eligibility, not automatic namespaces. For ingestion use a separate test account or verified disjoint product identities. Same-ID rehearsal requires a separate account or read-only validation: a second feed or a different ad destination in the same live account can overwrite the same item identity and is not safe staging. Confirm the replacement is accessible and the destination reports its ingestion, item-level acceptance and expected count; successful upload or fetch alone is insufficient. Only a separately authorised operator may change ownership or disable the old source. Avoid dual active writers with conflicting values. Record current source IDs, feed URL, schedules and rollback release; restore the previous source/feed configuration on a failed transition, without overwriting commerce or entitlement data. Verify destination-processed item identities and statuses after both the separately authorised switch and any rollback. Preserve existing feed URLs if routing/control actually permits it, rather than claiming this in advance.

### PR B: execution and qualification

1. Define the channel projection and adapter validation/export interfaces; add deterministic Google and Meta feed adapters and small merchant-operated CLI/reporting around existing build tooling. No new production channel credentials, external writes or daemon.
2. Build two merchant fixtures with different explicit IDs and routing conventions. Include variants, unchanged and intentionally changed image/price/eligibility, unavailable values, a browse-only item and an external/enquiry item. Assert no checkout entitlement follows from a feed entry.
3. Qualify deterministic bytes and schema rules per destination; invalid fields, unclear publication and ID reuse produce actionable diagnostics. Compare before/after snapshots and prove an unexplained change fails readiness, an approved exception is traceable, and cross-channel/account IDs do not alias.
4. Use authorised non-production channel accounts or provider validation facilities when available; otherwise report exactly what was verified locally and leave account ingestion as a rollout gate. Prove a documented source transition/rollback in a sandbox, including ingestion acknowledgment, not just a generated CSV.
5. Run repository regressions, CI and security checks; obtain exact-head independent review. No Woo feed removal or live channel cutover in this PR.

3. Qualify the end-to-end integration only after each focused PR's scoped checks pass. Keep real customer sending, backfill and production cutover disabled.
4. Freeze a merchant-approved, redacted day-one parity matrix covering every in-scope offer/variation, purchaser-visible resource names and order, target or file identity, access lifetime/download limits, recovery behavior, notification and overlapping workflows, plus channel IDs, grouping, URLs, price, availability and destination/market eligibility. Account for every scoped row; require zero unexplained differences or unauthorised grants, sends or feed changes. Verify mailbox receipt separately from provider acceptance and test fresh-browser access. Record how pre-cutover Woo links remain served, if promised; never bulk-resend historical purchases. Separately capture source-approved link lifetime, resource access rules, source/feed ownership and Google/Meta item IDs, account permissions, eligibility and destination-specific policies. Unknown values hold the affected rollout, not development using approved synthetic fixtures.

## Evidence and limits

- [Google product data specification](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/7052112?hl=en) and [Google unsupported Shopping content](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/6150006?hl=en-GB): field and destination rules must be rechecked against the merchant's actual account and target destination.
- [Meta feed onboarding documentation](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/facebook-business-extension/fbe/get-started/feed-onboarding): a scheduled pull feed is a supported integration pattern; this is not evidence of acceptance for this particular merchant or catalogue.
- [Cloudflare R2 presigned URLs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/api/s3/presigned-urls/): signatures expire between one second and seven days and are bearer capabilities, so durable customer access needs the merchant-authorised access layer described above.

No production migration evidence, feed inventory, current account eligibility or live access guarantees have been established by this plan.

## Independent Astra review — incorporated before implementation

Reviewed by GPT-6.2-Astra against immutable main `1da7226` and the actual dispatcher, email handler and migrations. Verdict: **revise before implementation**; the corrections above are incorporated in this plan. The reviewer identified concrete P0 failure cases: a v1 purchase can be matched to v2 workflow/resources; an interrupted claim can advance without grants or a reconstructable customer capability; and a previously issued R2 presigned URL can remain valid after revocation. The plan now pins order-time associations, separates durable grants/notification intent from sending, specifies proven-unsent replacement versus ambiguous holds, and uses an entitlement-checked streaming endpoint for v1.

The reviewer rechecked the revisions and approved the plan for implementation planning, subject to the included distinctions between SEO canonical and submitted variant URL, and Google product identity versus source/destination metadata. The reviewer also required a true third-provider test through the ordinary endpoint, channel account/market identity separation, isolated feed staging with item-level ingestion evidence, and a complete source-backed parity matrix. Presigning and generic API synchronization are deferred until a concrete need exists. Effective Policy source inventory, historical link commitments and channel-account eligibility remain unverified and are rollout inputs, not development assumptions.

Reviewer references: [Google product identity](https://developers.google.com/merchant/api/guides/products/add-manage), [Google data-source ownership](https://developers.google.com/merchant/api/guides/data-sources/api-sources), and [Cloudflare R2 presigned URLs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/api/s3/presigned-urls/).
