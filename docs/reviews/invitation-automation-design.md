# Review invitations: acquisition, consent and scheduled delivery

Status: design in progress; no new subscription, scheduler, sender or production activation. Reviews remains optional, independently installed and disabled by default. Historical import never produces an invitation. This is a proposed integration for a later implementation, not a change to checkout settlement or a general marketing platform.

## Existing boundaries

CartRay records `OrderConfirmed` in its outbox alongside the confirmed transition for both webhook and reconciliation paths. The outbox row is unique per order and event type; its `delivered_at` is not an independent acknowledgement per subscriber. An acquisition and its products are not themselves proof of recipient identity or permission to send. The current Reviews settlement reader, signed consent adapter, invitation generations and recovery dispatcher are explicitly test-only. The module declares no subscriptions or scheduled tasks.

Reviews owns its own database, invitation state and delivery decisions. Checkout must not depend on Reviews availability or wait for Reviews writes. An absent, disabled or read-only Reviews module causes no invitation-related checkout side effect. A production adapter must establish authenticated, narrowly scoped cross-service calls and explicit store/environment identity; passing an order ID from a request is not verification.

## Proposed flow

1. A durable CartRay outbox delivery mechanism emits the confirmed-order event with stable event identity and a schema version. The receiving adapter authenticates the origin and rechecks the authoritative confirmed acquisition, including webhook or reconciliation confirmation, paid eligible lines, the immutable manifest and a version-pinned product-to-review-subject mapping. Reject a conflicting or incomplete mapping; a zero-priced line is not verified purchase evidence. Do not change settlement predicates to make an event convenient.
2. A separately governed recipient adapter supplies a verified delivery destination and binding evidence for that acquisition. A purpose-specific permission adapter supplies an auditable grant for `review_invitation`, bound to the same recipient and acquisition. Neither a payment event, contact availability, nor a general marketing flag is sufficient. Unknown, absent, expired or revoked permission suppresses sending, while voluntary review access remains governed independently. Recipient addresses and sensitive evidence remain private and never enter public review projections or logs.
3. Persist an acquisition intake record with a stable identity scoped to store, environment and acquisition. Events delivered more than once or by different settlement paths must resolve to the same acquisition; reject an event with the same identity but changed immutable facts, and surface it for investigation. Never infer delivery exactly once from message delivery. Cross-database writes cannot be atomic: acknowledge only after durable Reviews-side intake, retry safely, and design replay/reconciliation around both sides. The existing single `outbox.delivered_at` must not be repurposed as the Reviews subscriber's acknowledgement where it could interfere with other consumers; independent delivery/checkpoint semantics are prerequisite work.
4. Apply an explicitly configured invitation policy to create a due-time intent. Default policy is off; first-send delay, any reminder count/timing and expiry of stale intents require merchant approval, and no reminder is assumed. Store the policy/version and due time with the intent, keyed by acquisition, intended purpose and attempt kind so duplicate event delivery or worker crashes cannot schedule duplicate invitations. Retries do not reset the clock or silently adopt a new policy. An immutable event identity is not a substitute for the acquisition identity.
5. At due time, a bounded queue-driven dispatcher claims the intent atomically with a lease/fencing identity. Re-read the confirmed acquisition, approved mapping, recipient binding, current consent, suppression and per-subject submitted state immediately before dispatch; resolve destination only from the trusted recipient adapter. If a prerequisite fails, do not send. Suppressed or stale work reaches an auditable terminal/held state, not an infinite retry. Pending work must not consume D1 through per-request scans or polling; use due-time/queue scheduling with a bounded, indexed reconciliation path for missed messages and expired leases. Design the maximum batch size, retry horizon and dead-letter policy before enabling it.
6. Reuse the existing invitation-generation and delivery-claim protocol. One independently random generation and durable delivery identity are prepared for a delivery; a generation becomes active only after authoritative provider acceptance. Its configured default lifetime is 60 days from that acceptance, not order time or job creation. Once a provider may have accepted a send, preserve `dispatching`/`uncertain` and reconcile by delivery identity with authoritative provider evidence; never blindly resend. A definitively failed send may be retried only under the existing safe policy. A replacement revokes the preceding capability without resetting completed acquisition/subject reviews. Reminder delivery, if approved, must not create a competing generation or reactivate revoked capability; its precise protocol must be designed against the existing generation state machine before implementation.
7. Submission or suppression before a queued reminder must cancel/suppress the appropriate reminder idempotently. Partial multi-subject purchases retain independent progress; do not assume submission of one subject completes all eligible subjects. Define and approve whether reminders stop after any submission or only when all eligible subjects are complete. A forwarded bearer link transfers the capability and does not establish personal identity. GET/HEAD must not consume it or create a review.

## Ownership, races and recovery

- Settlement, mapping, recipient evidence, consent and delivery are distinct authorities. No customer-controlled email address, form field or unsigned event can replace them. Record source event IDs and evidence versions privately with precise provenance; do not record raw tokens or addresses in telemetry.
- Recheck permission and suppression at final claim and immediately before the provider call. Define an explicit serialization/ordering rule for concurrent revocation and provider dispatch, including the unavoidable in-flight boundary; do not promise cancellation of a send already accepted by a provider. Receipt and status callbacks must be authenticated, replay-safe, scoped to the original delivery identity and never imply an unknown acceptance timestamp.
- Database uniqueness and guarded state transitions, not queue ordering, enforce one acquisition/subject review and one send claim per delivery identity. The reminder, replacement, withdrawal and submission paths must be tested concurrently. Unexpected facts, uncertain provider outcomes and exhausted retries enter a bounded operator-visible reconciliation queue, with evidence-backed actions and a stable staff audit actor. Retain the existing automatic recovery queue and delivery state machine; no parallel invitation scheduler may bypass them.
- Consent withdrawal, provider bounce/complaint or merchant suppression prevents future eligible sends; exact suppression scope, retention and appeal are policy decisions. Never recreate or re-enroll historical WordPress invitations.

## Implementation and release gates

A first implementation should qualify authenticated event intake, deduplication and due intent in synthetic Workerd/D1 before integrating a provider. Integration tests must cover confirmed webhook and reconciliation for the same order, duplicate/out-of-order messages, wrong store/environment, forged recipient or mapping, consent absent/revoked, partially completed purchases, crash before/after provider acceptance, replayed receipts, simultaneous replacement and scheduled dispatch, queue redelivery and no effect when Reviews is absent or disabled. Assert checkout remains available on every Reviews/provider failure and no delivery or customer record is produced from imported reviews.

A staging test must additionally prove the actual event transport and independent subscriber acknowledgement, authoritative recipient and consent evidence, provisioned scoped identities, sender and provider receipt lookup, schedule/lease and dead-letter operations, merchant moderation identity, privacy boundaries, monitoring and safe operator reconciliation. Production sending/collection remain unavailable until these, the approved reminder policy and the storefront withdrawal guarantee are separately qualified and explicitly activated. Permanent product mappings and the production release decision remain owner gates.
